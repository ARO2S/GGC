Members of the Greenville Garden Club gathered at the home of Bill and Sharon Ahern on N. Locust for an evening introduction to the art of bonsai. Bill shared what years of patient shaping, pruning, and training have taught him, giving members a real appreciation for just how much care goes into every miniature tree.

![Members settled in for an evening introduction to bonsai](/images/uploads/Bonsai%20(4).jpg)

The setting made the program even more memorable. The Aherns' backyard centers on a cascading water garden, complete with a stone waterfall, lily pads, and a quiet pond that ties the whole space together.

![The Aherns' backyard water garden, complete with a cascading waterfall and lily pads](/images/uploads/Bonsai%20(6).jpg)

Keeping a water feature that clean and calm takes more than luck. Tucked out of sight in a small utility room, a pump and plumbing system does the real work, quietly circulating water so the pond above can stay picture-perfect.

![The pump system tucked away behind the scenes, keeping the water garden circulating](/images/uploads/Bonsai%20(1).jpg)

The garden had plenty of whimsy to go along with the craftsmanship, including a hobbit-style door built right into a stone wall — the kind of detail that makes a garden tour fun for members of every age.

![Bill points out one of the garden's whimsical details, a hobbit-style door built into the stone wall](/images/uploads/Bonsai%20(3).jpg)

Penny Anaway put out a spread of snacks for the evening, with one of Bill's bonsai visible on the porch rail just behind her — a nice reminder of what the night was all about.

![Penny Anaway sets out refreshments, with one of Bill's bonsai visible on the porch rail](/images/uploads/Bonsai%20(2).jpg)

Thank you to Bill and Sharon for opening their garden and sharing a craft that takes real patience to master. Between the bonsai, the water garden, and the company, it was a beautiful evening.

![Bill Ahern chats with fellow members after the program](/images/uploads/Bonsai%20(5).jpg)
